# Bulova Lunar Pilot Review

Every watch brand has a story. Most of those stories involve heritage, craftsmanship, and tradition. The Bulova Lunar Pilot has a different kind of story: it went to the moon.

On August 2, 1971, astronaut David Scott wore a Bulova chronograph on his wrist during the Apollo 15 lunar surface EVA — making it one of only a handful of watches ever worn on the moon. The Lunar Pilot is the modern recreation of that watch, and it carries the most compelling narrative in affordable horology.

At $350–$450, the Lunar Pilot isn't just a watch. It's a conversation starter, a history lesson, and a gift with a built-in speech.

## The Story — Apollo 15 and the Moon

On July 26, 1971, Apollo 15 launched from Kennedy Space Center carrying Commander David Scott, Command Module Pilot Alfred Worden, and Lunar Module Pilot James Irwin to the moon. During the mission's lunar surface EVAs, Scott's government-issued Omega Speedmaster crystal cracked. He switched to his personal backup — a Bulova chronograph — which he wore for the remaining lunar surface activities.

That Bulova became one of the few privately owned objects ever used on the lunar surface. In 2015, the original watch sold at auction for $1.6 million.

The Lunar Pilot is Bulova's modern recreation of that watch. The case proportions, dial layout, and chronograph configuration are faithful to the 1971 original. The caseback is engraved with "LUNAR PILOT" and references to the Apollo 15 mission.

Why this story matters for gift-giving: every great gift needs a great story. "This watch went to the moon" is the kind of opening line that turns a gift presentation into a moment. Whether you're presenting it at a retirement party, a milestone birthday, or a graduation ceremony — that sentence lands.

## The High-Performance Quartz Movement

The Lunar Pilot uses Bulova's proprietary high-performance quartz (HPQ) movement, operating at 262,144 Hz — eight times the frequency of standard quartz movements. In practical terms:

- **Accuracy:** ±10 seconds/year (vs. ±15 seconds/month for standard quartz)
- **Chronograph precision:** 1/10 second measurement
- **Sweeping seconds hand:** The high frequency creates a smooth, sweeping motion rather than the tick-tick-tick of standard quartz
- **Battery life:** 3–4 years

The sweeping seconds hand is worth highlighting. Most quartz watches tick once per second — a dead giveaway of their quartz nature. The Lunar Pilot's 262 kHz frequency creates a continuous sweep that mimics mechanical watches. From across a table, you'd assume it's automatic.

The accuracy is extraordinary. ±10 seconds per year means you might adjust the time twice a year (for daylight saving) and that's it. For men who value precision — engineers, scientists, pilots, military — the HPQ movement is deeply satisfying.

## On the Wrist

The Lunar Pilot is a big watch. At 45mm with a 52mm lug-to-lug and 13.5mm thickness, it has commanding presence. This isn't a dress watch — it's a statement piece that works best on wrists 7" and above. On smaller wrists, it can feel overwhelming.

The leather strap version (96A225) at ~95g is more manageable for daily wear. The steel bracelet version (96B258) at ~180g adds heft and wrist presence but can feel heavy during extended wear.

The chronograph pushers are crisp and responsive — pushing start/stop/reset feels mechanical and satisfying. The tachymeter bezel around the dial is functional (for calculating speed based on time over a known distance) but most owners treat it as an aesthetic element.

The sapphire crystal is the spec that justifies the price premium. It's virtually scratch-proof, ensuring the Lunar Pilot looks pristine for years of daily wear. Given the watch's size and the likelihood of wrist impacts, sapphire is essential rather than optional.

**Comfort rating:** 7/10 (leather) / 6.5/10 (steel) — the size is the limiting factor, not the construction.

## FAQ

### Is the Bulova Lunar Pilot worth buying?

Yes — if the story resonates. The Apollo 15 heritage, high-performance quartz, and sapphire crystal justify the $350–$450 price. It's the most compelling narrative in affordable watchmaking.

### Is the Bulova Lunar Pilot a mechanical watch?

No — it uses Bulova's proprietary high-performance quartz (HPQ) movement at 262 kHz. However, the sweeping seconds hand mimics mechanical movement visually. Accuracy is ±10 seconds/year.

### Is the Bulova Lunar Pilot too big?

At 45mm with 52mm lug-to-lug, it's a large watch. Best suited for wrists 7" and above. If size is a concern, consider the 42mm Bulova Archive Series Lunar Pilot or look at alternatives like the Hamilton Khaki Field (38mm).

### Did the Bulova really go to the moon?

Yes. Astronaut David Scott wore a Bulova chronograph during Apollo 15's lunar surface EVA on August 2, 1971, after his government-issued Omega's crystal cracked. The original watch sold at auction in 2015 for $1.6 million.

### What battery does the Bulova Lunar Pilot use?

The HPQ movement uses a standard watch battery lasting 3–4 years. Replacement costs $15–$25 at most jewelers or watch repair shops.
