# Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 vs Quartz — Which Should You Gift?

*Same iconic design. Two very different movements. Here's how to choose.*

**Published:** April 10, 2026 · **By:** WristNerd Editorial Team · **Read time:** 6 minutes

---

## The Tissot PRX Dilemma

<!-- ~150 words -->

The Tissot PRX is one of the most popular watches in the world right now — and for good reason. The 1970s-inspired integrated steel bracelet, Swiss-made quality, and affordable luxury positioning make it an almost unfairly good gift.

But there's a choice to make: the PRX Quartz ($325–$375) or the PRX Powermatic 80 ($575–$650)? Same design language, dramatically different movements and price points.

This guide breaks down exactly which version makes the better gift — because the answer isn't always "buy the more expensive one."

## Movement: The Core Difference

### PRX Quartz

The Swiss quartz movement is accurate to ±15 seconds per month (vs ±15 seconds per day for automatics). It's thin, reliable, and requires a battery change every 2–3 years. For the recipient, it's "put it on and forget about maintenance."

### PRX Powermatic 80

The Powermatic 80 automatic movement is Tissot's flagship caliber — 80 hours of power reserve (best in class at this price), silicon hairspring for improved accuracy and longevity, and the satisfying sweep of a mechanical seconds hand. The exhibition caseback reveals the movement in motion.

**For gifts:** The Powermatic 80 adds emotional depth — the visible movement, the sweeping seconds hand, the ritual of wearing it to keep it wound. But if the recipient values simplicity and low maintenance, quartz is the smarter choice.

---

## Thickness & Wearability

This is where many buyers get surprised. The quartz PRX is **9.5mm thick** — sleek enough to slide under a dress shirt cuff with ease. The Powermatic 80 is **10.9mm** — noticeably thicker due to the automatic movement.

That 1.4mm difference sounds small on paper, but it changes how the watch sits on the wrist and under clothing. The quartz version feels like a true luxury-sport watch. The Powermatic feels slightly more substantial.

**For gifts:** If he wears suits or slim-fit shirts regularly, the quartz version's slimmer profile is a meaningful advantage.

---

## Value Analysis

### PRX Quartz ($325–$375)

At this price, you get a Swiss-made, sapphire crystal, integrated-bracelet watch that competes with designs costing 10x more. The [Tissot PRX](/reviews/tissot-prx/) offers arguably the best value in the sub-$400 Swiss watch market.

### PRX Powermatic 80 ($575–$650)

The [Tissot PRX Powermatic 80](/reviews/tissot-prx-powermatic-80/) adds the automatic movement and exhibition caseback for roughly $250 more. That premium buys a genuine mechanical watch with an 80-hour power reserve and Tissot's best caliber.

**For gifts:** The quartz version delivers better perceived value (Swiss + sapphire + integrated bracelet at $350 is remarkable). The Powermatic delivers better actual value per dollar spent if you value mechanical movements.

## Our Verdict: Which One to Gift?

### Gift the PRX Quartz if:

- Your budget is $300–$400
- He wears suits or slim-fit clothing regularly
- He values low maintenance and accuracy
- He's not particularly interested in watch mechanics
- You want the best perceived value (Swiss + sapphire + steel at $350)

### Gift the PRX Powermatic 80 if:

- Your budget extends to $550–$650
- He appreciates craftsmanship and mechanical things
- He'd enjoy seeing a movement through an exhibition caseback
- The occasion warrants a "step up" (milestone birthday, anniversary, promotion)
- He's already a watch enthusiast or aspiring to become one

### The Bottom Line

**Both versions are exceptional gifts.** The quartz PRX is one of the best values in watchmaking. The Powermatic 80 adds emotional depth and mechanical fascination. You genuinely cannot go wrong with either.

If budget is the deciding factor, buy the quartz with zero guilt. If budget allows the upgrade and the recipient would appreciate the difference, the Powermatic 80 is worth every extra dollar.

## FAQ

### Is the Tissot PRX Powermatic 80 worth the extra money?

If the recipient appreciates mechanical watches, yes — the 80-hour power reserve, sweeping seconds hand, and exhibition caseback add significant emotional value. If they just want a great-looking watch, the quartz delivers the same design for $250 less.

### Can you tell the difference between PRX Quartz and Powermatic 80?

From the front, the most visible difference is the seconds hand movement — the quartz ticks, the automatic sweeps. The Powermatic is also 1.4mm thicker. The caseback is the biggest tell: flat on the quartz, exhibition window on the Powermatic.

### Which Tissot PRX holds its value better?

The Powermatic 80 tends to hold value better in the secondary market due to the automatic movement. However, neither is a true "investment" watch — buy whichever makes the better gift.
