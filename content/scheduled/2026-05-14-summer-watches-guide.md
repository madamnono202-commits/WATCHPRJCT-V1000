## What Makes a Great Summer Watch

<!-- ~150 words -->

Summer changes the rules. Watches that work beautifully from October to April suddenly face enemies they weren't designed for: chlorine, saltwater, sand, sunscreen, sweat, and UV exposure. A great summer watch handles all of it while still looking good enough for rooftop drinks afterward.

Here's the minimum spec sheet for a summer watch:

- **100m water resistance** (minimum — 200m preferred)
- **Steel bracelet, rubber, or NATO strap** (leather absorbs water and sweat)
- **Sapphire or hardened crystal** (beach sand scratches mineral glass)
- **Luminous hands** (for late-night summer adventures)

## Summer Watch Care Tips

1. **Rinse after saltwater.** Saltwater corrodes gaskets and finishes over time. A quick freshwater rinse after ocean swims extends watch life significantly.

2. **Avoid leather in summer.** Leather absorbs sweat and water, leading to odor and deterioration. Switch to steel, rubber, or NATO for the season.

3. **Don't leave it in direct sunlight.** Extended UV exposure can fade dials and damage gaskets. Remove the watch before sunbathing.

4. **Check the crown before swimming.** Ensure the crown is pushed in (or screwed down, for dive watches) before entering water. An unsecured crown is the #1 cause of water damage.

## FAQ

### Can I swim with a 100m water-resistant watch?

Yes — 100m (10 ATM) is rated for recreational swimming and snorkeling. For diving, you want 200m (20 ATM). [See our water resistance guide →](/blog/watch-terms-glossary/)

### What's the best watch material for summer?

Stainless steel bracelets handle sweat and water best. Rubber and NATO straps dry quickly. Avoid leather in hot, humid conditions.

### Should I buy a separate "summer watch"?

If your main watch is under 100m WR or has a leather strap, a dedicated summer watch ($45–$110 from Casio or Citizen) is a smart and affordable investment.
