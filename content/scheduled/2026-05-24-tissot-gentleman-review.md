## First Impressions

<!-- ~200 words -->

The Tissot Gentleman arrives in Tissot's premium presentation case — matte black exterior, padded interior, magnetic closure. It sets the stage for what's inside, and what's inside doesn't disappoint.

The 40mm case is immediately commanding but never overwhelming. The sunburst dial — available in blue, black, green, and silver — has the kind of depth that photographs struggle to capture. In person, the radial brushing catches light at every angle, creating a sense of movement and sophistication that rivals watches twice the price.

The steel bracelet is where the Gentleman truly distinguishes itself from the competition. Brushed center links, polished outer links, and a butterfly deployment clasp with pushers — it's a level of finishing that usually costs $1,000+. The bracelet alone would sell this watch.

For a gift-giver, the Gentleman delivers what matters most: the moment the recipient puts it on and says "this feels expensive." Because it does. It just doesn't cost what it feels like it costs, and that gap between perception and price is the sweet spot of great gift-giving.

## Design & Build Quality

<!-- ~300 words -->

### The Dial

The sunburst dial is Tissot's calling card in the Gentleman line. The blue variant is our favorite for gifting — it's rich, versatile, and shifts from deep navy indoors to vibrant royal blue in sunlight. Applied indices are polished steel, and the dauphine hands are likewise mirror-polished, catching light with precision.

The date window at 3 o'clock is cleanly integrated with a matching frame. No magnifier — the date is easily readable thanks to the generous aperture and high-contrast printing.

### The Case

The 40mm stainless steel case hits the modern sweet spot. At 9.7mm thick, it's impressively slim for an automatic — thinner than most competitors with 80-hour movements. The case sides feature a combination of brushed and polished surfaces, with enough finishing detail to reward close inspection.

### The Bracelet

The 5-link bracelet is the Gentleman's star feature. The combination of brushed center links and polished outer links creates a visual depth that reads "luxury." The butterfly deployment clasp with push-button release is secure, easy to use, and comfortable. The micro-adjust in the clasp allows fine-tuning for comfort.

### The Crystal

Sapphire crystal with anti-reflective coating on both sides. This dual-sided AR is notable at this price — many competitors only coat the interior. The result is exceptional dial legibility from every angle, with virtually zero reflection.

**Build quality verdict:** Outstanding. The finishing, bracelet quality, and dual-sided AR sapphire place this firmly above its $575–$695 price tag. Comparable quality from Omega would cost $3,000+.

## Movement & Accuracy

<!-- ~200 words -->

The Powermatic 80.811 is Tissot's flagship caliber — and it earns that status. The headline is the 80-hour power reserve, which means he can take it off Friday evening and it'll still be ticking Monday morning. For someone who rotates watches or doesn't wear one every day, this is genuinely useful.

The silicon hairspring is the technical star. It's antimagnetic, resistant to temperature variations, and more durable than traditional metal hairsprings. In practical terms: the watch keeps better time for longer between services.

**Key movement specs:**

| Spec | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Type | Automatic (self-winding) |
| Frequency | 21,600 bph (3 Hz) |
| Jewels | 23 |
| Power Reserve | 80 hours |
| Hacking | Yes |
| Hand-winding | Yes |
| Hairspring | Silicon (antimagnetic) |

Accuracy on our test unit: approximately +4 seconds per day — excellent.

## Comfort & Wearability

<!-- ~150 words -->

At 40mm and only 9.7mm thick, the Gentleman wears like a dress watch that can do everything. It slides under shirt cuffs with ease — a significant advantage over chunkier automatics.

The bracelet is genuinely comfortable for all-day wear. The tapered design and butterfly clasp distribute weight evenly, and the micro-adjust allows precise sizing.

**Dress-up potential:** Excellent. This is one of the best dress-capable watches under $700.

**Dress-down potential:** Very good. The steel bracelet and clean dial work with jeans and a polo.

**Daily wearability:** Seven days a week. The 100m water resistance handles daily life confidently.

## FAQ

### Tissot Gentleman vs Tissot PRX — which is better?

Different watches for different men. The [PRX](/reviews/tissot-prx/) is sportier with its integrated bracelet and 1970s design language. The Gentleman is more refined, more versatile, and more traditionally elegant. For a dress-casual daily wear, the Gentleman wins. For modern style impact, the PRX wins.

### Is the silicon hairspring worth it?

Yes — it improves accuracy, reduces servicing frequency, and adds magnetic resistance. At this price point, it's a significant technical advantage.

### How does the Tissot Gentleman compare to the Longines Conquest Classic?

Both are excellent Swiss automatics. The Gentleman offers better technology (80-hour PR, silicon hairspring) at a lower price. The Longines offers stronger brand prestige and heritage. Choose based on whether you value specs or brand cachet.
