# 5 Affordable Watches That Actually Hold Their Value

*A great gift today. Still worth something tomorrow.*

**Published:** April 30, 2026 · **By:** WristNerd Editorial Team · **Read time:** 5 minutes

---

## Why Value Retention Matters for Gift-Givers

<!-- ~150 words -->

Here's a reality most gift guides won't tell you: most watches depreciate the moment you buy them. A $300 fashion watch might be worth $30 on the secondary market in two years. That's not necessarily a dealbreaker — watches are meant to be worn, not traded — but it's worth knowing.

The watches on this list are different. They maintain strong resale values because of brand reputation, movement quality, cult followings, or all three. If the recipient ever decides to sell, trade, or upgrade, these watches return a meaningful percentage of the original investment.

That's not why you should buy them. You should buy them because they're excellent watches. But value retention is a nice bonus — and a sign that you chose well.

---

## Our Top 5 Value-Retaining Affordable Watches

---

### 1. Casio G-Shock GA2100 "CasiOak" — $80–$110

**Resale retention:** 70–90% of retail

The [Casio G-Shock GA2100](/reviews/casio-g-shock-ga2100/) has become one of the most sought-after watches in the affordable space. The "CasiOak" nickname (after its Royal Oak-inspired octagonal design) has given it cult status. Limited colorways routinely sell for above retail, and even standard versions maintain strong resale values.

**Why it holds value:** Cult following, limited colorways drive demand, virtually indestructible construction means used examples are often near-mint.

---

### 2. Seiko 5 SRPD55 — $220–$270

**Resale retention:** 60–75% of retail

The [Seiko 5 SRPD55](/reviews/seiko-5-srpd55/) benefits from Seiko's enormous collector community. The 5 Sports line is endlessly moddable — bezel inserts, dials, bezels, straps — which creates secondary demand even for stock examples. The Seiko name carries weight in every watch market on earth.

**Why it holds value:** Seiko brand loyalty, modding community demand, proven 4R36 movement, broad appeal across all demographics.

---

### 3. Tissot PRX — $325–$375

**Resale retention:** 65–80% of retail

The [Tissot PRX](/reviews/tissot-prx/) has been the watch industry's biggest success story of the past three years. The integrated steel bracelet, Swiss-made pedigree, and $350 price point have created insatiable demand. Used examples in good condition regularly sell for $250–$300 — remarkable retention for a quartz watch.

**Why it holds value:** Ongoing hype cycle, Swiss-made cachet, distinctive design that's instantly recognizable, limited competition at its price point.

---

### 4. Hamilton Khaki Field Mechanical — $400–$500

**Resale retention:** 65–75% of retail

The [Hamilton Khaki Field Mechanical](/reviews/hamilton-khaki-field-mechanical/) is a modern classic with decades of military heritage. Swiss-made, hand-wound, and positioned at the entry point of "serious" watchmaking. Collectors respect the lineage, and the simple hand-wound movement is easy and inexpensive to service.

**Why it holds value:** Swiss-made prestige, military heritage, enthusiast respect, Hamilton's strong brand positioning within the Swatch Group hierarchy.

---

### 5. Orient Bambino V2 — $130–$170

**Resale retention:** 55–70% of retail

The [Orient Bambino V2](/reviews/orient-bambino-v2/) is the most recommended entry-level automatic dress watch on every watch forum, subreddit, and YouTube channel. That constant exposure creates steady demand. Used examples in good condition sell for $80–$120 — strong retention for a sub-$170 watch.

**Why it holds value:** Ubiquitous recommendation engine, in-house automatic movement, no real competition at its price point for automatic dress watches.

## What Makes a Watch Hold Value?

1. **Brand reputation.** Seiko, Casio, Hamilton, Tissot, and Orient are all established, respected names. Fashion brands (MVMT, Daniel Wellington) depreciate rapidly.

2. **In-house or respected movements.** Watches with Seiko, Orient, or ETA movements maintain demand because parts and service are readily available.

3. **Cult following or community.** The G-Shock modding community, Seiko modders, and Tissot PRX hype all create secondary-market demand.

4. **Timeless design.** Trendy designs depreciate when the trend ends. Classic designs maintain appeal indefinitely.

5. **Quality construction.** Sapphire crystal, solid cases, and reliable movements age gracefully. Cheap materials age poorly.

## FAQ

### Should I buy a watch as an investment?

No — at least not at these price points. Buy watches as gifts and personal accessories. Value retention is a bonus, not a strategy. If you want investment-grade watches, you're looking at Rolex, Patek Philippe, and Audemars Piguet territory ($5,000+).

### Where can I check resale values?

WatchCharts, Chrono24, eBay completed listings, and r/Watchexchange are the best resources for real-world resale pricing.

### Do quartz watches hold value?

Generally worse than automatics — but the Casio G-Shock and Tissot PRX are notable exceptions, proving that cult status and design triumph movement type.
